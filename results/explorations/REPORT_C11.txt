1. DESIGN (code: c11_lib.py, c11_run.py; analysis c11_analyze.py; all constants in c11_lib.P)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Station terms c (station, phase), fixed per network, used as the corrections of the detectors:
  S0    registered +SC (median of the gated peak delays of the 20 catalogue events at the catalogue hypocentres; +-1 s
        joint P/S read, energy gate 25).
  S1    SC-R of C10 (c10_screloc.sc_reloc, unchanged).
  S2    JHD on the 20-event catalogue. Start: terms = S0, events at the catalogue hypocentres. 3 passes; pass 1 uses
        the registered read; passes 2 and 3 re-read every event within +-0.5 s of the prediction of its current
        hypocentre and terms, energy gate 12. In each pass: (a) alternation (at most 8 times, stop below 0.002 s):
        residual scale = 1.4826 MAD of the gated residuals (floor 0.05 s); each event with >= 4 gated arrivals is
        relocated by iteratively reweighted Gauss-Newton (Huber 1.345, homogeneous straight-ray times, Gaussian prior
        N(0, 1 km) per coordinate and N(0, 0.1 s) on the origin around the catalogue hypocentre); gauge: median
        origin shift of the relocated catalogue events = 0; term = median over events of (read time - travel time -
        origin); (b) joint Gauss-Newton over all terms and relocatable hypocentres (at most 15 iterations, Cauchy
        weights 2.385, the same priors), then the same gauge.
  S3    self-calibration: MT-FW (cap 6) + T3 with the S2 terms over the 30 unlabelled windows of the network; atoms
        with selection score >= the 0.80 quantile of the stream's atom scores (30 per network); each atom is an extra
        event with prior N(0, 3 km) / N(0, 0.3 s) around the refined atom, read within +-0.5 s of its own prediction
        (gate 12), used with >= 6 gated arrivals (realised: 13.6 per network in thin clutter, 18.5 in strong); catalogue
        events (S2 state) and atoms then go through 2 more passes of the S2 procedure. One iteration.
  S4    S2 with the first 40 / 80 catalogue events (S2@40, S2@80).
  ORA   true statics (as C10 MT-ORA; timing level = c10_lib.sigma_oracle, 0.05 s).
Detectors: MT-FW (cap 6) and BOMP-PK loops of c10_lib.py, unchanged; hard +-0.25 s peak rule; T3 = the frozen C10
  refinement (1 km lattice, 10 km, origins within 1 s, penalised peak search, record score = selection statistic).
  Timing level of T3 for a term set: the C10 catalogue rule recomputed with that term set (realised medians, thin /
  strong: S0 0.144 / 0.220, S1 0.105 / 0.178, S2 0.141 / 0.208, S3 0.140 / 0.205, S2@40 0.132 / 0.206, S2@80 0.130 /
  0.210; ORA 0.049 / 0.048).
Run set: MT family S0, S1, S2, S3, S2@40, S2@80, ORA, each hard and +T3; BOMP family S0 and S2 hard and +T3, ORA +T3.
Deviations from the wording of the task (all written in the plan before the evaluation):
  - gauge: not a zero mean per phase. The true statics are not zero-mean and a common offset moves origin times, which
    the strict hit rule sees; absolute time is tied to the catalogue origins and the weak directions are held by the
    catalogue prior (1 km / 0.1 s, the known catalogue error);
  - relocation by prior-regularised Gauss-Newton instead of a grid search;
  - joint Gauss-Newton stage added after the noise-free validation (section 3);
  - BOMP-PK only with S0, S2 (the primary candidate) and ORA (+T3 only for ORA): cost. HUBER-N not run (not cheap: each
    term set needs its own operator tables in c8_lib). CEIL-GD not run.

What mattered: the gate of the re-read. With the registered gate (25, i.e. 5 noise sd) only about half of the arrivals
of a catalogue event are used and many 4-station events cannot be relocated; reading again around the predicted time
with a gate of 12 brings in the weaker arrivals safely. Window width (0.3-1.0 s), number of passes, robust constants,
prior width 0.5 km and minimum number of arrivals changed the error by less than the network-to-network noise (0.01 s);
a weaker position prior (2 km) is worse (0.118 / 0.186 with the joint stage): the estimate leans on the catalogue prior.
S3 variants (20-40 windows, quantile 0.6-0.9, atom prior 1.5-6 km, first read +-1 s, a self-consistency filter): 0.056-
0.068 / 0.104-0.126, never better than S2 by more than 0.01 s in the sum.

10. JUDGEMENT
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Rules of the plan. (A) primary contrast >= +0.05 with a 95% interval excluding 0 and no loss in dense windows (point
>= -0.01 under LOC+T and LOC): +0.070 [+0.010, +0.120]; dense +0.065 and +0.034. MET. (B) fraction of the oracle gain
>= 0.5 with the numerator's interval excluding 0: 0.77 [0.18, 1.45]. MET.
  S2 (JHD, 20 events): PLAUSIBLE CONTRIBUTION, as the calibration half of a pair. Rule-fixed, catalogue-only, 0.4 s
     per network. It gives nothing with the hard peak rule in isolated events; it is what makes the T3 refinement
     work, and in strong clutter it is the difference between T3 doing nothing (+0.009) and T3 being useful (+0.084).
  S3 (self-calibration from detections): NOT SHOWN to add to S2 (+0.016 [-0.019, +0.064] STD, -0.009 DENSE; term error
     0.069 against 0.072). Its point estimates in standard windows are the highest of the 20-event estimators (+0.085
     [+0.035, +0.140]), but the plan named S2 by a rule fixed beforehand and the difference is noise. The
     self-calibration idea is therefore not supported here; the reason is visible in the development set: half of the
     selected atoms are false and the true ones are located to about 2 km, so 14-18 usable atoms add little to 19
     relocatable catalogue events.
  S1 (SC-R): NOT REPLICATED in standard windows (+0.023 [-0.050, +0.077]); positive in dense (+0.042 [+0.014, +0.073]).
  S4: more catalogue lowers the term error steadily; the recall gain over 20-event JHD is not resolved (+0.01 to
     +0.02).
  ORACLE: with T3, +0.091 (STD) and +0.078 (DENSE) over the registered terms under LOC+T; nothing with the hard rule
     in standard windows.
Caveats that must travel with any number above.
  (i)   The effect exists under the origin-time hit rule. Under the registered rule (LOC) the standard-window contrast
        is +0.017 [-0.028, +0.068] and the oracle gives the same; a claim about the registered endpoint is not
        supported. In dense windows it is positive under both rules.
  (ii)  It is conditional on T3; "station terms improve the detector" is not what was measured. What was measured is
        that the origin-time-consistent recall of the refined detector depends on the terms, and that a JHD of the same
        20 events recovers most of what the true statics would give.
  (iii) One seed, 30 networks per cell and regime, a primary interval whose lower end is +0.010. C10 effects measured on
        20 networks shrank on these 30 (T3 with +SC from +0.066 to +0.024; oracle hard from +0.087 to +0.020). Expect
        the same of +0.070.
  (iv)  The BOMP-PK family shows the same direction with an interval touching 0 (+0.057 [-0.003, +0.115]).
  (v)   The JHD leans on the catalogue prior (1 km / 0.1 s, taken as known). A network whose catalogue errors are
        larger or unknown is not covered; with a 2 km prior the development error was back at the S0 level.
  (vi)  Statics sd 0.3 s, the 20-event catalogue and the +-1 s peak rule of +SC are our design choices; the size of the
        S0 error, and so the room for improvement, depends on them.
  (vii) In strong clutter the 20-event JHD is worse than S0 in a third of the networks (term error), although the
        pooled recall gain is the same as in thin clutter.
  (viii) The oracle arm uses a timing level of 0.05 s in T3 and the candidates 0.13-0.21 s (the C10 rule); the terms and
        the level are confounded in the oracle; since S2@80 and the oracle agree (+0.004 [-0.037, +0.047]), the level
        does not seem to matter at 0.25, consistent with C10 section 6.1.

11. NOVELTY
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Not new: joint hypocentre determination with station terms (Douglas; Pujol; Frohlich), source-specific station terms
(Richards-Dinger and Shearer), static corrections by iterated relocation, robust (L1 / Huber) residual statistics,
catalogue priors in the relocation. S2 is a small Bayesian JHD; it is new only in its data: it never uses a pick, it
reads the matched-filter energy peak around the predicted time and re-reads it as the hypocentres and terms move.
Self-calibration from one's own detections is the standard bootstrap of template-matching and of iterative
association-relocation workflows.
What would be new if it stands: (a) station terms estimated without picks and used INSIDE a pick-free moment-tensor
subspace detector, with (b) a measured effect on detection at a fixed false-alarm rate, next to the bound given by the
true statics, and (c) the finding that the effect appears only together with a local refinement by the timing
likelihood and only when a hit must have the right origin time. (d) The negative on self-calibration from detections
(no gain over a 20-event JHD at this purity and location accuracy) is reportable with its mechanism. None of this is a
new detector; it is a calibration result about what limits one.

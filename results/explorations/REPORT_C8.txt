0. SUMMARY
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(1) A fact about the generator that changes the question. gen_realistic draws the site factors exp(N(0, 0.5)) per
    station and phase AT EVERY CALL, i.e. anew in every window and in every catalogue event. They are not station
    terms. In the world where 2F was confirmed ("IID"), catalogue-estimated amplitude terms cannot work by
    construction. I therefore ran two worlds: IID (the default generator, unchanged) and PERS (the same factors fixed
    per network, shared by catalogue and windows: the physically meaningful case).
(2) Catalogue amplitude terms (SITE-CAT) do not replace the cap. IID: SITE-CAT - MT-FW(cap 6) = -0.066 [-0.108,
    -0.028]. PERS: -0.040 [-0.078, +0.007]; the terms recover a small, uncertain part of the oracle gain (SITE-CAT -
    uncapped +0.020 [-0.014, +0.048]; oracle +0.052 [+0.012, +0.104]). With the cap added, SITE-CAT-CAP equals MT-FW
    (+0.006 [-0.015, +0.043]).
(3) Removing the TRUE site factors (ORACLE, no cap) reaches MT-FW only in PERS at sigma 0.5 (-0.008 [-0.049, +0.055])
    and falls short elsewhere (IID 0.5: -0.011 [-0.056, +0.044]; sigma 0.25: -0.044; sigma 0.8: -0.043). The cap still
    adds +0.03 to +0.09 on top of the oracle. At sigma 0.25 the oracle gains nothing over BOMP-PK (-0.001) while the
    cap still gains +0.043. So site amplification explains part of the cap's effect, not all of it.
(4) The best principled variant is HUBER-N: a Huber M-estimate of the moment tensor on the station/phase residual
    norms with the threshold fixed at 2.7955 noise sd (sqrt of the chi-square(3) 0.95 quantile). Dense windows,
    sigma 0.5:  HUBER-N - MT-FW    IID +0.022 [-0.014, +0.055]   PERS +0.005 [-0.025, +0.048]
                HUBER-N - BOMP-PK  IID +0.064 [+0.028, +0.105]   PERS +0.066 [+0.024, +0.105]
    Headroom captured (IID): HUBER-N 0.21 [0.09, 0.39], MT-FW 0.14 [0.01, 0.29]. Standard windows: HUBER-N - BOMP-PK
    -0.014 [-0.050, +0.042] (IID) and -0.005 [-0.077, +0.070] (PERS); the cap gives -0.041 [-0.086, +0.029] in IID.
    BUT at sigma 0.8 HUBER-N is below the cap: -0.043 [-0.113, -0.001] (still +0.076 [+0.011, +0.136] over BOMP-PK).
(5) Threshold sensitivity (post hoc sweep): the HUBER-N gain over BOMP-PK is flat from 1.5 to 3.4 noise sd (+0.059 to
    +0.091), halves at 5 (+0.043) and vanishes at 8 (+0.004). The cap's window is narrower and has a harmful low end
    (cap 3: -0.050; 6: +0.042; 8: +0.028; 12: -0.025). HUBER-N does not remove the dependence on a level; it widens
    the usable range about twofold and takes the level from the noise distribution instead of a calibration.
(6) The other principled variants fail: HUBER-S (scale-adaptive threshold), GAIN / GAIN-FREE (per-block gains with or
    without a log-normal prior) and NORM-TR (trace normalisation) are all below MT-FW and close to BOMP-PK.
Judgement: MODEST, PLAUSIBLE design contribution for HUBER-N ("matches MT-FW cap 6 at the nominal site spread without
a calibrated cap, and does not hurt isolated events"), NOT a contribution for the site-term idea itself. See section 9.

1. DESIGN (code: c8_lib.py; runner c8_run.py; analysis c8_analyze.py)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Worlds (c8_lib.generate_site = gen_realistic.generate_real copied so that the site factors are returned and can be
overridden; same rng consumption):
  IID   site factors redrawn at every call (default generator). sigma 0.5 (main), 0.25 and 0.8 (sensitivity).
  PERS  site factors exp(sigma z) fixed per network (station, phase), z from rng [999, 3809, ci, j], shared by both
        regimes, used by the catalogue and by every window.
  In both worlds the P and S factors of a station are independent (the generator's own choice), so "site factors that
  differ between P and S" is the default. The opposite case (P = S) was planned and not run.
Detector engine (c8_lib.mtfw_general): the c6_lib.mtfw_dense loop (energy peak search on the residual, statistic over
the whole node x origin lattice, running minimum, 5 km exclusion, deflation of the fitted prediction at the peak
samples) with a pluggable statistic. v_b is the 3-component matched-filter vector of station/phase block b in noise-sd
units, G the radiation operator of the candidate node, Q an orthonormal basis of G.
  MT-FW, MT-FW-NOCAP, MT-FW-C3/C8/C12   ||Q^T v||^2/2 with |v_b| clipped at 6 / not clipped / at 3, 8, 12.
  ORACLE, ORACLE-CAP    the same with the rows of G multiplied by the TRUE site factors of the window (the exact
                        whitened GLRT with known gains); without and with the cap 6. Not a detector: a bound.
  SITE-CAT, SITE-CAT-CAP   rows of G multiplied by exp(u), u the catalogue amplitude terms per station and phase.
  SITE-CAT-ST           one catalogue term per station (both phases).
  HUBER-N               T = sum_b rho_c(|v_b|) - min_a sum_b rho_c(|v_b - Q_b a|), rho_c the Huber function of the
                        block residual NORM (quadratic below c, linear above); a by IRLS, 4 passes from the LS fit;
                        deflation with the IRLS fit. c = sqrt(chi2_3 quantile 0.95) = 2.7955 in noise-sd units: a
                        pure-noise block is left unweighted with probability 0.95. No cap on the data.
  HUBER-S               the same with c = 2.7955 x max(1, median_b |r_b| / 1.5382), the robust scale of the LS
                        residual of the candidate (concomitant scale).
  GAIN                  profile likelihood with a positive gain per block and a log-normal prior of sd 0.5 (the
                        generator's nominal value): T = |v|^2/2 - min_{m,u} [sum_b |v_b - e^{u_b} G_b m|^2/2 +
                        sum_b u_b^2/(2 0.25)]; alternating, 3 rounds. GAIN-FREE: gains free and >= 0 (only the
                        polarisation direction and sign of each block are modelled).
  SITE-CAT-GAIN         GAIN with the prior centred on the catalogue terms (PERS designs only).
  NORM-TR               each station trace divided by its peak amplitude in the window (times the median peak), then
                        the uncapped statistic. Back-projection practice; heuristic.
  BOMP-PK               registered BOMP-PK+SC (task2d_methods.bomp_pk_record).   CEIL-GD   verif_2e ceiling (c6_ceil).
Catalogue amplitude terms (c8_lib.site_terms): per catalogue event, peak times by c6_lib.pick_event (+-1 s around the
homogeneous prediction plus the +SC correction), the 3-component vector at the peak, gate energy >= 25 (the +SC gate);
l = log|v| - log A, A the RMS amplitude of the model over 300 random double couples at the catalogue location;
event term (median over the event's gated blocks) removed; u = median over events (>= 3 picks, else 0), re-centred;
3 rounds.
Designs and sample:
  DENSE-IID   j 60-79, 5 windows: 600 windows, 2410 events      STD-IID   j 60-79, 4 windows: 480 windows, 368 events
  DENSE-PERS  j 60-79, 4 windows: 480 windows, 1919 events      STD-PERS  j 60-69, 4 windows: 240 windows, 195 events
  DENSE-IID-S25, DENSE-IID-S80   j 80-89, 4 windows: 240 windows each (970 / 960 events); no CEIL-GD.
  Supplements on the DENSE-IID windows (records of MT-FW and HUBER-N regenerated identically, 1200/1200 and 600/600):
  HSWEEP (all 600 windows; c = 2.5003 and 3.3682, the 0.90 and 0.99 quantiles), HWIDE (j 60-69, 300 windows; c =
  1.5, 5, 8).

2. WHAT WAS FIXED BEFORE THE RESULTS AND WHAT WAS NOT
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Fixed before any detection run: all method definitions above, including the HUBER-N constant 2.7955 (rule: chi-square
  0.95 quantile for a 3-component block, noise sd = 1), the HUBER-S scale rule, the GAIN prior sd 0.5, the designs.
  No HUBER constant was tried on any window before the DENSE-IID run. HUBER-N was NOT tuned on seed 999.
Chosen on seed-999 data, without detection results: the catalogue amplitude estimator. c8_sitedev.py compared four
  estimators on the catalogues of networks 60-63 (PERS, thin) against the true site factors (correlation 0.42 for a
  per-event moment-tensor ratio, 0.74 for the radiation-averaged one, which was kept). Networks 60-63 are part of the
  evaluation set, so SITE-CAT in the PERS designs is mildly favoured. It does not matter for the conclusion (SITE-CAT
  is null).
Post hoc, after seeing the DENSE-IID results:
  (a) HUBER-N had been left out of STD-IID, STD-PERS and the sensitivity designs to save compute; it was put back
      before those designs ran. HUBER-N was not declared the primary beforehand: I had named HUBER-S as the primary
      Huber variant (standard concomitant scale). HUBER-N is therefore the best of 11 candidates on DENSE-IID, and its
      DENSE-IID estimate carries a selection bias. DENSE-PERS, STD and the sigma designs are other windows (and partly
      other networks), run with the same frozen definition.
  (b) HSWEEP and HWIDE (the threshold sweeps) are post hoc.
  (c) The sensitivity arm was cut from 4 designs to 2 (IID sigma 0.25 and 0.8) because of the interruption.
  (d) MT-FW-C4 was dropped before the run (time). 11 candidates x 6 designs: no multiplicity correction anywhere.

9. JUDGEMENT
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Criterion: plausible contribution if a principled variant matches or beats MT-FW (cap 6) without a tuned cap, and/or
captures a substantial fraction of the CEIL-GD headroom.
  Site terms (the physics idea of the task): NOT MET. In the confirmed generator the site factors are not station
    terms at all, so there is nothing for a catalogue to learn. Where they are (PERS), 20 catalogue events on 4-8
    stations give terms that correlate 0.72 with the truth, cover 76% of the blocks and recover +0.020 [-0.014,
    +0.048] of an oracle gain of +0.052; the uncapped SITE-CAT stays -0.040 below the cap. Even the oracle only
    equals the cap, and the cap adds to the oracle. Site terms do not replace the cap; with the cap they add nothing
    measurable (+0.006 [-0.015, +0.043]).
  Nuisance gains with a prior (GAIN, GAIN-FREE, SITE-CAT-GAIN), scale-adaptive Huber (HUBER-S), trace normalisation
    (NORM-TR): NOT MET. All below MT-FW by 0.03-0.08; NORM-TR loses 0.13 on isolated events.
  HUBER-N: MET at the nominal spread, with qualifications. It matches the cap in three dense samples at sigma <= 0.5
    (+0.022, +0.005, +0.002; never significantly different), beats BOMP-PK by +0.064 [+0.028, +0.105] and +0.066
    [+0.024, +0.105], captures 0.21 [0.09, 0.39] and 0.16 [0.06, 0.28] of the headroom against 0.14 and 0.15 for the
    cap, and shows no cost on isolated events in STD-IID (-0.014, 90% interval inside +-0.05) where the cap has
    -0.041. Its threshold comes from a textbook rule and the result is flat over 1.5-3.4 noise sd.
    Qualifications: (i) it was not the pre-named primary, so DENSE-IID carries selection among 11 candidates; (ii) it
    is behind the cap at sigma 0.8 (-0.043 [-0.113, -0.001]); (iii) it has a level too, only a wider and better
    motivated one: the gain is gone at 8 noise sd; (iv) no interval for HUBER-N - MT-FW excludes 0 in its favour, so
    the claim is equivalence, not superiority; (v) it captures about a fifth of the headroom, like the cap: the
    remaining four fifths are untouched by any amplitude treatment, the oracle included; (vi) the hit rule ignores
    origin time (2F verification, section 2e), which inflates dense recall for every method alike.
Novelty caveats. Station amplitude terms are standard in magnitude estimation and in amplitude-based location; Huber
and other M-estimators are standard in moment-tensor inversion and robust stacking. What would be new is only the
measured statement: inside a moment-tensor subspace certificate used for detection with deflation, bounding the
influence of each station/phase block is what pays in dense sequences, it can be done on the residual with a
noise-derived constant instead of a calibrated clip on the data, and explicit site terms (estimated or even known) do
not substitute for it. This is a design result on one synthetic generator, not a new method.
Recommendation: do not pursue the site-term variants. If the thesis wants to remove the "arbitrary cap 6" objection to
2F, confirm HUBER-N as an equivalence claim against MT-FW and a superiority claim against BOMP-PK (section 10), and
report the sigma 0.8 weakness as a registered secondary arm. Keep MT-FW cap 6 as the reference detector: it is 9 times
cheaper and at least as good at every spread tried.

Question: does a moment-tensor certificate with a per-station/phase Huber-weighted fit and a noise-scaled threshold
  fixed by rule (HUBER-N) (a) beat BOMP-PK+SC in dense windows, (b) match MT-FW+SC with cap 6 there, and (c) leave
  isolated-event recall unchanged?
Data: seeds 5151 and 6262 (independent replications). Networks task2a_confirm.network(seed, cell, j), j = 0..79 (80
  per cell and regime), 3 cells, regimes thin and strong; +SC from 20 catalogue events as in 2A; default gen_realistic
  (site factors redrawn per window, sd 0.5); 5 dense windows (c2_run.draw_dense, kmax 7) and 5 standard windows
  (draw_sources, kmax 5) per network from two new TAGs. 2400 dense and 2400 standard windows per seed.
Methods (code frozen and hashed: c8_lib.py, c8_run.py, c6_lib.py, task2d_methods.py, xc.py):

  mtfw_general "huber_n": Huber function of the residual norm of each station/phase 3-vector, threshold c = sqrt of the
  chi-square(3) 0.95 quantile = 2.7955 in matched-filter noise-sd units, 4 IRLS passes from the LS fit, statistic =
  Huber loss of the data minus Huber loss of the residual, deflation with the robust fit. The constant was set by this
  rule before any run and was not tuned; a post hoc sweep on seed 999 (c = 1.5 to 8) is disclosed in REPORT_C8.txt.

  point per method; hierarchical bootstrap (strata regime x cell, networks then windows, 2000 replicates); pooled over
  regimes and cells, per seed.

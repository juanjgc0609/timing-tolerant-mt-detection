0. SUMMARY
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(1) The hypothesis as posed is NOT supported. Charging the peak shifts a Gaussian timing log-likelihood (T1, T1c, T2,
    T2w, T4), with the level fixed by rule from the catalogue, gives no recall gain under either hit rule, in either
    window type, in either detector. Against MT-FW at 0.25, registered rule: dense -0.028 to -0.002 (e.g. T2w -0.002
    [-0.046, +0.033]), standard -0.066 to -0.006. Against BOMP-PK: dense T1 -0.010 [-0.033, +0.007], T2w -0.017
    [-0.044, +0.020]; standard T1 +0.034 [-0.022, +0.069], T2w +0.017 [-0.059, +0.085]. A sweep of fixed levels 0.05,
    0.10, 0.20 s (half sample, section 6) changes nothing. The 5-10 km near-miss share does not move (dense 0.204 ->
    0.203-0.224; standard 0.212 -> 0.187-0.248).
(2) T4 answers the "hard cut or flatness" question in the direction opposite to the hypothesis: widening the free
    window to +-0.5 s LOSES (T4h dense -0.047 [-0.087, -0.008]; LOC+T -0.049 [-0.072, -0.022]) and the penalty only
    takes back part of the loss (T4 dense -0.019 [-0.063, +0.017], standard -0.066 [-0.142, +0.000]). The +-0.25 s cut
    is doing useful work; its flat interior is not what loses the events.
(3) Why (sections 3 and 7): on a 3 km lattice the within-cell travel-time spread (rms 0.18 s for P, 0.30 s for S) is
    already as large as the tolerance, and the +SC corrections of a 20-event catalogue are wrong by 0.12-0.24 s rms
    (measured against the true statics; the true model error is 0.05-0.07 s). The timing budget of a real hypothesis is
    therefore about 0.2-0.4 s per arrival, not the 0.144 s of the Cramer-Rao table, and a prior with that honest width
    is flat inside +-0.25 s. The Cramer-Rao bound of C9 P5 holds for KNOWN statics.
(4) ORACLE (true statics, true model-error level 0.05 / 0.07 s): the gain exists and is large.

(5) The one candidate with a signal is T3, local refinement of each selected atom on a 1 km lattice by the joint

    their own times (SC-R) cut the correction error from 0.145 to 0.110 s in thin clutter (0.214 to 0.202 s in strong).

(7) First-iteration diagnostic (section 7): before any deflation half of the isolated events lose to a false node; the

Judgement: a soft timing prior inside the detector is not a plausible contribution in this benchmark as long as the
station corrections come from a 20-event catalogue; the plausible contribution suggested by C10 is on the calibration
side (how well the statics are known), with T3 as a small, conditional location / origin-time refinement. Sections 9-11.

1. DESIGN (code: c10_lib.py, c10_run.py; analysis c10_analyze.py)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Shift of an arrival: delta_bi = (chosen sample) x DT - (d_i / V_b + tau + corr_bi), i.e. relative to the CONTINUOUS
predicted time (the registered code truncates the prediction to a sample; the fractional part is kept here).
All variants keep the registered lattice, origins, corrections, kmax, exclusion, deflation and running-minimum record;
only the choice of the sample per station and phase and the node statistic change. Statistic units: s = ||Q^T v||^2 / 2
(MT-FW, vectors capped at 6) or e / 2 = h^T K(I)^+ h / 2 (BOMP-PK), i.e. log-likelihood-ratio units at unit noise.
  B     hard      registered rule: energy peak within +-4 samples (+-0.25 s), no charge. BOMP-PK, MT-FW.
  T1    indep     sample maximising E(k) / 2 - delta^2 / (2 s2_bi) (E capped at 36 in the MT-FW family, as its vectors
                  are); statistic minus sum_bi delta^2 / (2 s2_bi); s2_bi = sig^2 + cell term of the node, cell term =
                  variance of the travel time for an event uniform in the lattice cell and the origin step:
                  [(n_x^2 + n_y^2) 3^2 + n_z^2 4^2] / (12 V_b^2) + 0.1^2 / 12 (rms 0.18 s P, 0.30 s S).
  T1c   indepc    the same with s2 = sig^2 only: the literal reading of the hypothesis (catalogue spread, no cell term).
  T2    corr      common-mode constraint. Prior covariance of the shift vector of a node C = sig^2 I + J S J^T, J = d t /
                  d (x, y, z, tau) of the straight-ray model, S = diag(3^2, 3^2, 4^2, 0.1^2) / 12: the shifts are a
                  location / origin-time term already searched plus independent residuals. Peaks by the T1 rule;
                  theta = S J^T C^-1 delta; peaks re-chosen once among the same samples with (delta - J theta)^2 / (2
                  sig^2); statistic minus delta^T C^-1 delta / 2. (This is the closed form of "a finer origin-time and
                  position grid with penalised residual shifts"; a literally finer origin grid was not run.)
  T2w             T2 with a +-0.5 s window (the validation showed that +-0.25 s truncates the S shifts of an off-node
                  event, which reach +-0.7 s on a 3 km lattice, so that T2 at +-0.25 s is not self-consistent).
  T3    refine    selection by the hard rule; the selected atom is then moved to the maximum of the T1 objective on a
                  1 km lattice (same three depths; cell term of the 1 km cell, rms 0.10 s P, 0.16 s S) within 10 km
                  (epicentral) and origins within 1 s, outside the exclusion zones of earlier atoms; ties and no gain
                  keep the atom. Exclusion and deflation use the relocated atom. Record score = the selection statistic
                  (fixed before the runs); "/sref" = the same atoms scored by the refined objective (secondary; the
                  selection statistic where an atom had no refinement candidate).
  T4    indep     T1 with a +-0.5 s window.     T4h   hard rule with a +-0.5 s window, no charge.
  ORACLE          true statics as corrections; sig = sd per phase of the TRUE model error (travel time in the
                  generator's gradient medium minus the homogeneous travel time, station median and event mean
                  removed, over the 20 true catalogue sources): median 0.05 s P, 0.07 s S. MT-ORA (hard), MT-ORA+T2w,
                  MT-ORA+T3.
Timing level sig, RULE (fixed on the catalogues of validation network 195, before any detection run): robust sd
  (1.4826 MAD about zero) of the +SC peak delays of the gated (energy >= 25) catalogue arrivals minus the station
  correction, event median removed (catalogue origin-time error), pooled over stations and both phases; floor 0.05 s.
  Realised values (120 network x regime): thin median 0.138 s (quartiles 0.108-0.178, range 0.076-0.263); strong
  0.224 s (0.185-0.269, range 0.128-0.391).
Main run set (trimmed to the compute budget after timing the validation; the dropped variants ran only there):
  MT-FW, MT+T1, MT+T1c, MT+T2, MT+T2w, MT+T3, MT+T4, MT+T4h, MT-ORA, MT-ORA+T2w, MT-ORA+T3;
  BOMP-PK, BOMP+T1, BOMP+T2w, BOMP+T3; CEIL-GD (verif_2e ceiling, c6_ceil) on every window.
Diagnostics per method: median epicentral error of the hit atoms (all atoms, LOC rule; bootstrap over networks);
  near-miss share = fraction of true events that are not hit and whose nearest FALSE atom lies 5-10 km away (the
  "capturing atom at 5-10 km" of the task, made operational this way because in dense windows the nearest atom of an
  event is often the hit of its neighbour).
Fraction of the headroom = (method - BOMP-PK) / (CEIL-GD - BOMP-PK) at 0.25, LOC rule, ratio bootstrapped. CEIL-GD has
  no origin-time version, so no headroom is quoted under LOC+T.

7. WHY: THE TIMING BUDGET, AND FIRST-ITERATION SEPARABILITY
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7.1 Budget of the shift of a true arrival relative to the prediction of its nearest node (s, rms):
      true model error (gradient medium against homogeneous rays, after a perfect station term)   0.05 P, 0.07 S
      error of the +SC corrections against the true statics (median removed; networks 160-179)    thin 0.12-0.17,
                                                                                                   strong 0.18-0.24
      position inside the 3 km x 3 km x 4 km cell and the 0.1 s origin step                        0.18 P, 0.30 S
      the same for a 1 km lattice                                                                  0.10 P, 0.16 S
      +-0.25 s tolerance as a uniform law (the "0.144 s" of C9 P5)                                 0.144
    The off-node S shift reaches +-0.7 s. On this lattice the +-0.25 s window is not a generous tolerance that wastes
    information; it is narrower than the spread it has to absorb, and what it "discards" is mostly the cell term,
    which a prior cannot remove (only a finer lattice or a refinement can). The Cramer-Rao figure of 0.4-0.7 km assumes
    known statics and a continuous position. With corrections wrong by 0.12-0.24 s the same table (C9 section 6: 0.44-
    0.72 km at 0.15 s, 0.84-1.34 km at 0.30 s) still gives 0.4-1.1 km, well inside the 5 km hit radius: the bound does
    not explain the lost events either. Timing errors of this size do not blur the location of an event that is found;
    they lower the statistic of its node against false nodes supported by clutter and coda (7.2), and the correction
    error is a fixed bias per station, not noise that a longer stack averages out.

  give the best non-oracle result of the study in standard windows (LOC @0.1 +0.102 [+0.028, +0.196], @0.25 +0.064

10. NOVELTY
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Soft timing tolerances are old. Probabilistic location (Tarantola-Valette; Lomax's NonLinLoc with Gaussian model
errors and the equal-differential-time likelihood), back-projection / source scanning with Gaussian or boxcar
travel-time uncertainty windows, "stacking with time uncertainty" in migration-based detectors, and joint hypocentre
determination with station terms all contain the idea that a shift costs a likelihood. T2 is the textbook Gaussian
marginalisation of position inside a cell; SC-R is JHD with a prior. Nothing in the form of the prior is new.
What would be new, if it worked, is the place it is put: a timing prior INSIDE a moment-tensor subspace detector with
deflation (the charge competes with the projection energy of a 6-parameter amplitude model at every node and origin
and travels through the exclusion and deflation loop), with the level FIXED BY RULE from catalogue residuals instead
of tuned. C10 says that this combination does not pay in this benchmark at the catalogue level, and why. The reportable
results are (a) the negative with its mechanism: on a coarse lattice with catalogue-grade station terms the timing
budget is 0.2-0.4 s and a principled prior is flat inside the tolerance; (b) the conditional positive: with statics
known to the model-error level the same prior is worth +0.08 to +0.19 recall and reaches the realistic ceiling in
isolated events; (c) refinement by the timing likelihood improves origin time and location of isolated events where
refinement by time-free evidence (C9) destroyed them. None of the three is a method claim.

3. NULL CALIBRATION AND TUBE FORMULA (out/c12_null.txt)

3.1 Derivation. In Q coordinates the datum of a candidate is y = Rm m a + z, z ~ N(0, I_6) with a fixed reading. The
    generalised likelihood ratio over a set of directions U of the sphere S^5 is max_U (u . y)^2.
    U = S^5 gives chi2 with 6 df; U = trace-free hyperplane, chi2 with 5 df; U = {Rm m / |Rm m| : m double couple} is a
    closed manifold of dimension 3 (the orbit of SO(3), with a stabiliser of order 4, symmetric: -m is also DC).
    Hotelling-Weyl / Gaussian kinematic formula for a closed manifold of dimension 3:
        P(max_U u . z > t) ~ L3 rho3(t) + L1 rho1(t),   rho3 = (2 pi)^-2 (t^2 - 1) exp(-t^2 / 2),
                                                        rho1 = (2 pi)^-1 exp(-t^2 / 2),
    L3 = vol(U), L1 = (1 / 4 pi) integral of the scalar curvature S; L2 = 0 (no boundary) and L0 = Euler characteristic
    = 0. S is obtained from the Gauss equation, S = 6 + |H|^2 - |II|^2, with the second fundamental form computed from
    the second derivatives of the orbit. L3 and L1 are integrated by Monte Carlo over Haar rotations
    (c12_lib.lk_curvatures). Check of the form: with the sphere S^3 (L3 = 2 pi^2, L1 = 3 pi) the expression gives
    exactly the tail of chi2 with 4 df, (1 + x / 2) exp(-x / 2).
    Reading: the dominant term is that of 4 degrees of freedom (3 angles plus the amplitude) multiplied by
    L3 / vol(S^3), which equals 2 in the isotropic metric. The DC manifold "occupies" two spheres S^3, and that is why
    the effective degrees of freedom are 4.4-4.5 and not 4.

      site factor sd 0.5 (generator)                  1.62 / 2.25
      site 0.5 + polarisation 10 degrees (generator)  1.66 / 2.46
    The amplitude errors of the generator (lognormal site factor with sd 0.5 per station and phase, polarisation with
    10 degrees) take the signal off the DC manifold and cost 0.7-1.5 units more than the exact model, growing with the
    amplitude. That explains 1.7-2.5 of the 5.9-6.4 units observed. The other 3-4 were NOT decomposed: peak reading
    at stations where the arrival is weak (noise, coda or clutter is read, which under the peak rule has twice the

    against homogeneous operators. It is a reasonable hypothesis, not a result.
6.3 Size of the ceiling. Even with perfect conservation, +3.4% to +4.5% of amplitude (section 3.6) is little: the slope
    of recall with amplitude across terciles (0.06, 0.32, 0.57 for median amplitudes in an approximate ratio 1 : 1.4 :
    2) is about 0.7 per unit of logarithm, which would give +0.02 to +0.03 of recall. The interval observed for DC
    ([-0.021, +0.033]) does not exclude that ceiling; what rules it out as an expectation is 6.1. The slope estimate
    is crude.

8. VERDICT AND LIMITS
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
NULL for detection. None of the four rules (DEV5, DC-proj, MIXED, nor the two extras) changes recall at a fixed
false-event rate, in standard or dense windows, with LOC+T or with LOC, in any regime or cell. The weak isolated events
are not recovered. It is not a negative result: there is no loss (the point estimates of DC are mostly positive,
+0.00 to +0.03) and the cost is low.
POSITIVE for the theory. The null calibration of the DC statistic is derived and verified: tube formula with two
constants computable per node, 4.4-4.5 effective degrees of freedom, threshold 10% lower at equal false-alarm rate.
It is a clean and reusable result (it gives calibrated p-values for a double-couple test and quantifies that the DC
hypothesis is worth at most 5% of amplitude), but it is not a detection lever on this test bench.
No frozen configuration and no preregistered confirmation are proposed. With the measured paired sd (0.0134 standard,
0.0071 dense, 16 networks per cell and regime) detecting a true effect of +0.01 with power 0.8 would require some 225
networks per cell and regime in standard windows or 63 in dense ones; this is not justified by an effect whose
theoretical ceiling is +0.02 to +0.03 and whose mechanism (6.1) points to zero.
Limits of this study.
  (i)   One seed, 16 networks per cell and regime. The intervals rule out gains larger than +0.03 (standard) and
        +0.015 (dense), not gains of +0.01.
  (ii)  Only the detector with the hard rule and +SC corrections; it was not tested with the T3 refinement nor with
        the JHD terms of C11. With better timing and position the signal would lie closer to the DC manifold and the
        loss of 6.1 could drop; this is not measured.
  (iii) The deflation always used the 6-parameter fit. Deflating with the DC fit was not tested.
  (iv)  The generator imposes site factors of sd 0.5; with calibrated sites the amplitude loss (6.2) would drop
        from 1.7-2.5 to 1 unit. That would improve the balance by 1 unit out of 45, 2% of energy: that alone does
        not change the conclusion either.
  (v)   The null with strong clutter is not Gaussian; the theory gets the threshold ratio right (0.89-0.90) but not
        for a demonstrated reason: it is an empirical regularity of this clutter (isotropic directions).
  (vi)  No multiplicity correction: 5 contrasts, 2 designs, 2 rules, 2 caps, 9 groupings = 360
        intervals. The 6 that exclude 0 (one in 4.3, five in 5, all in terciles) are read as noise or
        redistribution.
What remains of the idea. (a) The analytical piece, for the thesis as the calibration of a mechanism test. (b) The
observation that the amplitude margin given by any restriction of the source subspace is about 5% at most: the
37-39% of isolated events missed because of amplitude (C10) is not recovered along this path. (c) The MIXED rule
as a possible cheap re-scoring of weak events in dense sequences (+0.01 to +0.02 in the lower tercile), which
only deserves follow-up if it appears again in another study without having been looked for.

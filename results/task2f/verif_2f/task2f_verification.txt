2b. Scoring: the FROC uses one threshold per method over pooled windows, so score scale differences between methods

    Duplicate atoms on an already-hit event count as neither hit nor false. That is the 2A-2E rule, and it does
    not favour MT-FW. Counting duplicates as false changes MT-FW - BOMP-PK at 0.25 from +0.072/+0.051/+0.074/+0.043
    to +0.068/+0.042/+0.075/+0.043 (thin 4242, strong 4242, thin 8888, strong 8888; points_verif_out.txt).
    MT-FW's duplicate counts are similar to or above BOMP-PK's (e.g. 829 vs 847, 903 vs 771).

    (diag_verif_out.txt). kmax = 7 does not create the effect.

2e. Hit rule (hitrule_verif_out.txt). The registered rule ignores origin time, and in dense windows all events lie

      5 km & |dt| <= 0.5 s : +0.045 / +0.024 / +0.043 / +0.027

5a. Cap level (MT-FW minus BOMP-PK, cap 0.1 / 0.25, thin+strong pooled; BOMP-PK 0.110/0.208):
      CAPV 2  -0.064/-0.122   CAPV 3  +0.004/-0.056   CAPV 4  +0.040/+0.017   CAPV 5  +0.058/+0.047
      CAPV 6  +0.074/+0.070   CAPV 8  +0.058/+0.046   CAPV 12 +0.033/+0.010   CAPV 24 -0.003/-0.008   none -0.003/+0.001
    The effect has a single peak at about 5-8 noise-sd units, with its maximum exactly at the fixed CAPV = 6. It is
    harmful at 3 or less and gone at 24. CAPV = 6 was fixed in Task 1x (cal_capv.py, a different generator, standard
    windows) as the best of {6, 12, 24, none}. No value below 6 was tried. It was not tuned on dense windows or on
    4242/8888, so there is no leakage. But the confirmed gain belongs to this cap level, not to "a per-station cap"
    in general. The data do not say how a user would choose the level without calibration. At CAPV 5 or 8 the seed
    999 gain (about +0.047) would still clear the 0.03 bar.
5b. Clutter removed (n_clutter = 0): the gain INCREASES. MT-FW(6) - BOMP-PK = +0.137 [+0.058, +0.183] and
    MT-FW(6) - NOCAP = +0.139 [+0.073, +0.190] at 0.25, against +0.070 [+0.016, +0.131] and +0.069 [+0.023, +0.128]
    with clutter. Clipping clutter spikes is therefore NOT the cause. The regime difference (thin > strong) comes
    from clutter partly defeating the cap.
5c. Clutter AND lognormal site factors removed (site_sd = 0): BOMP-PK recall rises from 0.180 to 0.296 at 0.25, and
    the cap's gain shrinks to +0.023 [-0.040, +0.069] at 0.25 (+0.055 at 0.1). CAPV 12, 24 and none are then equal to
    BOMP-PK. Most of the gain is the cap robustifying the detector against per-station amplitude factors that its
    radiation model does not contain (generator site factors lognormal, sigma 0.5). This agrees with the pilot
    report's stated mechanism ("site-amplified arrivals dominate the unclipped statistic"). The size of the gain
    therefore depends on the generator's assumed site-amplification spread.
5d. Selection vs score (swap variants on the same residuals, 0.25, pooled; 5b values in brackets):
    - Selecting with the cap and scoring uncapped: +0.039 over BOMP-PK (+0.076).
    - Selecting uncapped and scoring with the cap: +0.025 (+0.093).
    - The full cap: +0.070 (+0.137).
    Both parts contribute and they combine more than additively. On the confirmatory data (diag_verif_out.txt), the
    within-window, threshold-free gain is small: unique hits among the first k atoms rise by +0.001 to +0.012. The
    first-atom hit rate rises by +0.02 to +0.05 (e.g. 0.833 vs 0.795). The largest difference is in the reliability
    of high scores: among the top 20% of first-atom scores, MT-FW is correct 0.90-0.96 of the time and BOMP-PK
    0.79-0.84. The uncapped first-atom scores spread 5x (p90/p10) against 2x capped. Much of the FROC gain at 0.25
    false/window is therefore better cross-window calibration of the statistic under a single global threshold.
    That is legitimate for the registered endpoint, which is a single-threshold detector. It is not purely
    "resolving overlapping events more correctly within a window", so the 2F report's wording should say so.

7. HONEST SCOPE OF THE CLAIM
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Supported: in this synthetic generator (gen_realistic, homogeneous detector model, catalogue station corrections,
lognormal site factors sigma 0.5, thin/strong Ricker clutter), in windows with 3-5 events clustered within 9 km, MT-FW+SC
with the per-station/phase cap CAPV = 6 has higher recall than BOMP-PK+SC at <= 0.25 (and <= 0.1) realised false events
per window. The gain is +0.04 to +0.07 under the registered location-only hit rule, and +0.02 to +0.05 when the
origin must also match within 0.5 s. It replicates in two untouched seeds and both clutter regimes. Removing the cap
removes the gain, and putting it into BOMP-PK's selection statistic recovers most of it.
Not supported or not tested:
  (i) any real-data claim;
  (ii) "a per-station cap" at an arbitrary level: the effect needs a level near 5-8 noise-sd units and reverses at
      3 or less;
  (iii) robustness to clutter as the mechanism: it is not; the mechanism is mainly robustness to unmodelled
      per-station amplitude (site) factors, plus better cross-window score calibration;
  (iv) absolute detection quality: recall stays 0.22-0.29, and about half of the location-only "hits" have origin
      errors above 0.5 s;
  (v) no harm on isolated events in strong clutter: a cost of about 0.03 is not excluded.
Novelty: clipping, normalising or sign-stacking per-station contributions is established robust-stacking and
back-projection practice. The defensible contribution is empirical: inside a moment-tensor subspace detector with
deflation, the clip level interacts with unmodelled site amplification and sets how well dense sequences are
detected under a global threshold, while joint refitting and the P/S Gram term do not matter. It is a measured
design result, not a new method.

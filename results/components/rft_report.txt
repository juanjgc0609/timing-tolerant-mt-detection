RANDOM-FIELD THRESHOLD FOR THE MAXIMUM OF THE MOMENT-TENSOR CERTIFICATE - Component A
Code: rft_threshold.py (null_max_samples, fast_field, Marginal, roughness, lkc_box, ec_density_chi2,
eec_threshold, eec_time_threshold, bonferroni_threshold, gev_threshold, conformal_threshold, validate,
selftest_eec; `python rft_threshold.py [out.txt]` reruns everything, about 6 min on one core)
======================================================================================================

SET-UP
* Statistic T(theta) = ||Q(theta)^T v(theta)||^2 / 2 exactly as fields_x (WAVE-MT) and fw_record (first
  Frank-Wolfe score), computed by a vectorised fast_field (max |difference| to fields_x 5e-14).
  M = max over 675 nodes x 31 origin times = 20,925 hypotheses.  In an empty window the MT-FW catalogue at
  threshold u is non-empty iff M > u, so alpha = P(M > u) is the per-window false-alarm probability.
* Cells: random 4, random 6, one-sided 8 stations (task1r_fair.SCEN).  Two network settings: 'fixed' (one
  network, make_network(kind, n, 25999), what a real deployment sees) and 'per_window' (a new network per
  window, the task1x/1y protocol).  Pure Gaussian noise = generate_y with no source and no clutter.
  Calibration: 300 windows (seed stream 100000); held-out test: 300 windows (seed stream 200000); clutter
  (fixed networks): 35 pulses of amplitude 0.35 or 1.75, 300 calibration + 300 test windows each, separate
  seed streams.  In total 7,200 noise windows.
* Yardstick.  The naive Clopper-Pearson interval of the realized rate ignores the randomness of the
  calibration set.  For an EXACTLY calibrated split-conformal threshold with n_cal = n_test = 300 the realized
  rate is beta-binomial, with 95% predictive band [0.00, 0.03], [0.02, 0.09], [0.057, 0.15], [0.18, 0.32] at
  alpha = 0.01, 0.05, 0.10, 0.25.  Both checks are reported; the band is the fair one.

1. MARGINAL OF T AT A SINGLE HYPOTHESIS (i)
  2T would be chi2_6 if v were white Gaussian without selection.  It is not: the max-energy sample of a
  9-sample window of narrow-band matched-filtered noise inflates each block.  At single hypotheses and pooled,
  mean(2T) = 12.6-13.5 (chi2_6: 6), var = 35-50 (12), q.99 = 29.9-34.5 (16.8), q.999 = 33-49 (22.5);
  KS distance to chi2_6 = 0.52-0.58.  A scaled chi-square fitted by moments, 2T ~ c chi2_k with c = 1.3-1.9 and
  k = 7.1-9.6, fits the bulk (KS 0.010-0.045) but overstates the upper tail (at the pooled 0.999 quantile it
  gives ~2x the exceedance).  Above its 0.999 quantile the tail is T ~ (0.54-0.68) chi2_(10.6-14.0), i.e. lighter
  than the moment fit (a Gumbel-like tail from the max-of-window selection).  The cap (energy 36) is never
  reached by Gaussian noise.  The marginal hardly depends on the network (pooled 2T mean 13.0-13.1 in all
  cells).  The rft code therefore uses the pooled empirical CDF with a chi-square tail fitted above the
  0.999 quantile, and maps T to its chi2_6 equivalent T* = F_chi2_6^{-1}(F_T(T)).

2. EXPECTED EULER CHARACTERISTIC (ii)
  * The EC densities rho_0..rho_4 of a chi2_6 field (Worsley 1994) and the LKC of a box are verified by
    simulation of a smooth chi2_6 field on a 10^4 lattice: EEC 7.007 / 4.664 / 1.874 against mean lattice EC
    6.98 / 4.62 / 1.70 (+-0.26 / 0.18 / 0.12) at u = 12 / 16 / 20, and EEC 0.158 against P(max > 28) = 0.12.
    rho_1 also matches the Rice upcrossing formula.  The machinery is correct.
  * Roughness of the certificate field (lag-1 correlation of T*): x 0.13-0.20, y 0.09-0.18, z 0.09-0.21
    (3 km, 3 km, 4 km steps), tau 0.51-0.63 (0.1 s step).  Adjacent nodes are nearly independent, because
    moving 3 km shifts S times by up to 1 s against a +-0.25 s window.  Lambda_tautau = 46-68 s^-2; the only
    sizeable off-diagonal terms are z-tau (correlation +0.25-0.27) and, in the one-sided network, x-tau
    (-0.17 to -0.20).
  * LKC from roughness: L4 = 1.6-3.1 x 10^4, more than the 20,925 lattice points.  The continuum field has
    far more "room" for excursions than the lattice.  Measured on the data, the effective number of independent
    hypotheses (alpha / P(T > u_conformal) at alpha = 0.05) is 8,700-19,000; the continuum EEC implies
    330,000-640,000.
  * Result: EEC with roughness-based LKC (EEC-rough) overestimates P(M > u) by 20-60x.  Thresholds are
    5-9 units too high and the realized false-alarm rate is ~0.002 when 0.25 is requested (ratio 0.01).
    Two lattice-aware variants are still conservative by ~4x: EEC-time (union bound over nodes x 1-D EEC /
    Rice in tau) and EEC-ECfit (LKC fitted to the empirical lattice EC curve, then extrapolated).  Realized
    0.012/0.029/0.074 for 0.05/0.10/0.25.  Bonferroni with the empirical marginal is the least conservative
    analytic predictor (realized 0.021/0.047/0.108; ~0.43 of nominal) and is a valid upper bound.

4. CLUTTER (35 pulses, no source; fixed networks)
  * Weak clutter (0.35) barely moves the maximum (mean +0.0 to +0.3).  Gaussian-calibrated GEV / conformal
    thresholds stay inside the band (12/12 each), with a slight excess at small alpha: pooled realized
    GEV 0.016/0.050/0.115/0.261, conformal 0.015/0.034/0.127/0.264 for 0.01/0.05/0.10/0.25.
  * Strong clutter (1.75; visible, picked transients) shifts the maximum by +8 to +9 (mean 31.6/33.8/34.3
    against 22.5/25.0/26.4) and doubles its sd (4.2-4.9).  EVERY threshold calibrated on Gaussian noise fails,
    including the random-field ones: realized 0.60/0.78/0.90/0.96 (conformal) and 0.14/0.27/0.35/0.47 (the
    very conservative EEC-rough) for 0.01/0.05/0.10/0.25.  That is 17-22x nominal for the empirical ones.
  * Re-calibrating on separate clutter windows with the same clutter law restores calibration for the
    empirical predictors: GEV 0.005/0.056/0.111/0.263, conformal 0.004/0.062/0.102/0.281, Gumbel
    0.002/0.043/0.092/0.263; 12/12 inside the band for each.  The alpha = 0.05 threshold rises from ~28-31 to
    ~39-43.  Re-estimating marginal and roughness on clutter windows does not rescue the EEC: still
    conservative, by 3-5x (EEC-time, EEC-ECfit) to >50x (EEC-rough).

FRANK ASSESSMENT
  * Calibrated: split-conformal (finite-sample guarantee P(M > u) <= alpha under exchangeability) and the GEV
    fit to window maxima (smooth, and it can extrapolate below 1/n_cal), both from ~300 noise-only windows of
    the SAME network and noise/clutter regime.  Gumbel is calibrated but slightly conservative at small alpha.
  * Not calibrated: the random-field predictors.  They are conservative (safe) on Gaussian noise, by 20-60x in
    probability for the continuum EEC with roughness-based LKC and ~2-4x for the lattice-aware variants and
    Bonferroni.  They are anti-conservative by an order of magnitude once non-Gaussian clutter is present.
  * The claim of Section 7.5 point 4, that the EEC of a chi2_6 field "fixes the false-alarm rate per window
    before any data are seen", is NOT supported, for three separate reasons:
      (1) The marginal is not chi2_6 (the max-energy selection doubles the mean and changes the tail shape);
          a data-estimated quantile transform is needed.
      (2) The field decorrelates within one grid step in space, so the continuum EEC counts 15-30x more
          effective tests than the lattice has.
      (3) Non-Gaussian transients shift the maximum far beyond any Gaussian prediction.
    What survives: the EEC/Bonferroni bound as a conservative prior threshold, and the empirical correction
    (GEV or conformal on noise-only windows) that the proposal already names as the fallback.  That fallback
    is the primary method, and it must be re-run whenever the noise/clutter regime changes (the correction is
    only as good as the exchangeability of calibration and operational windows).
  * Caveats: 300 calibration + 300 test windows per setting (alpha = 0.01 is barely resolved: 3 expected
    exceedances); one fixed network per cell; synthetic white Gaussian noise; clutter law identical in
    calibration and test.

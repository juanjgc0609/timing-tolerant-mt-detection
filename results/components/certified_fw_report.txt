CERTIFIED ORACLE FOR FRANK-WOLFE (MT-FW) - Component B
Code: certified_fw.py  (certified_fw_record, certified_oracle, cell_bounds, node_tau_bounds, leaf_enclosure,
check_bound; `python certified_fw.py [out.txt]` reruns the verification below, about 6 min on one core)

SUMMARY
* certified_fw_record(STA, MFn, grid, taus, Gs, Qs, truth, kmax=5, min_sep=5.0, mode=...) returns EXACTLY the
  records of task1x_mtglrt.fw_record (bitwise equal (score, hit) lists) in 72 of 72 windows of
  task1y_clutter.generate_y (pert; weak and strong clutter; random 4, random 6, one-sided 8 stations; seed-999
  streams), in all three modes: 'lipschitz' (the bound of Appendix Z), 'box' (same bound with exact
  station-to-cell distance ranges) and 'enclosure' (default; subspace-aware leaves).
* The energy bound is VALID (proof below; 0 violations on 48 residual fields x 300 random cells, raw and
  deflated, and on 1,004,400 single hypotheses) but it is TOO LOOSE TO PRUNE: over the 72 windows it avoids only
  4% of exact node evaluations (215,982 of 225,444) and, with the bound bookkeeping, is 3x SLOWER than the dense
  loop (172 s vs 56 s).  It prunes only when a strong event sets a high incumbent (first iteration, 4 stations:
  70% of nodes evaluated); in the noise-dominated later iterations it evaluates 93-100% of nodes.  Median
  (cell bound / true cell maximum) = 4.6-5.0.
* With a subspace-aware leaf enclosure (the statistic recomputed in batch plus a rigorous floating-point error
  bound; see 2), exact evaluations with fw_record's own call fall to exactly one node per Frank-Wolfe iteration
  (360 vs 225,444, 626x fewer) and wall time falls 4.3x (13.1 s vs 55.9 s).  Frank accounting: this mode still
  recomputes the statistic for ~100% of hypotheses (6.97 M batched evaluations); the saving comes from
  CERTIFIED VECTORISATION, not from pruning the hypothesis space.

1. THE BOUND AND WHY THE CAP, THE ARGMAX AND DEFLATION DO NOT BREAK IT
  T(theta) = ||Q_g^T vt(theta)||^2 / 2, vt = per station i and phase b the signed 3-vector R_i[:, k*] at the
  energy argmax k* of the window W(theta) = {clip(floor(t_bi(theta)/DT) + o) : |o| <= 4}, capped at |v| <= 6.
  (a) Q_g has orthonormal columns, so ||Q_g^T w|| <= ||w||:  T <= (1/2) sum_bi ||vt_bi||^2.
  (b) ||vt_bi||^2 = min(36, E_i(k*)) = min(36, max_{k in W(theta)} E_i(k)), E_i(k) = ||R_i[:, k]||^2: the
      selected sample has the largest energy of its window and the cap only rescales it.  Since
      e -> min(36, e) is non-decreasing, for any W' containing W(theta):  ||vt_bi||^2 <= min(36, max_{W'} E_i).
      So the argmax selection and the cap cannot break the bound: the selected vector of any sub-window has
      energy <= the maximum energy of the enlarged window, hence capped norm^2 <= min(cap^2, max energy).
  (c) For a cell with centre c, spatial radius h and origin-time half-width dtau, the triangle inequality
      (the eikonal Lipschitz property; 1/V_min in a heterogeneous medium) gives |t_bi(theta) - t_bi(c)| <=
      h/V_b + dtau for every theta in the cell; floor and clip are monotone, so every window of the cell lies in
      [clip(floor((t_c - D)/DT) - 4), clip(floor((t_c + D)/DT) + 4)].  UB(cell) = (1/2) sum_bi min(36, max
      energy in that enlarged window) >= max_cell T.  Range maxima in O(1) by a sparse table.  The 'box' variant
      uses [dist(station, box), farthest corner] instead of |c - s| +- h (a subset, hence also valid).
  (d) Deflation: the bound only uses the energies of the CURRENT residual R.  After each Frank-Wolfe step the
      fitted contribution is subtracted, E and the sparse table are rebuilt from the new R, and (a)-(c) apply
      verbatim.  Verified directly on deflated residuals (section 6 of the tables).
  (e) Floating point: +1e-6 s on window limits and x(1 + 1e-9) + 1e-12 on bounds.
  Why the energy bound cannot be much tighter: any bound that uses only per-block energies must dominate
  max{||Q^T v||^2 : ||v_bi|| = a_bi}, which equals sum a_bi^2 whenever range(Q) contains a vector with those
  block norms.  Under noise the true T is ~ (6 / 6ns) of ||v||^2, so the bound exceeds typical values by a
  factor ~ns and exceeds the global maximum of noise-dominated iterations (T ~ 19-37) at most hypotheses.
  Tightening requires the directions of the candidate vectors, which is what the enclosure uses.

2. EXACTNESS AND THE ENCLOSURE
  * Best-first search, pruning only cells whose bound is STRICTLY below the incumbent, so every node carrying a
    hypothesis equal to the global maximum is evaluated.  The winner is chosen with fw_record's tie rule
    (ascending node index, first tau, strict improvement).  Exclusion (min_sep), deflation and the record are
    fw_record's code.
  * Exact evaluation must use fw_record's own call (node_vectors over all 31 taus, then vf @ Qs[g]): BLAS matmul
    rounds a row differently depending on the number of rows (measured: bit mismatches in 5-90% of random
    trials for subsets of 1-16 rows of a 31-row product).  Evaluating tau subsets would change the last bits
    of the scores and could flip near-ties.  So a node is the unit of exact evaluation (x31 hypotheses).
  * Enclosure mode: on cells of <= 16 nodes, the statistic of every hypothesis is recomputed in batch (einsum)
    from the same argmax and cap tables.  Both floating-point evaluations of ||Q^T v||^2/2 are within 60u||v||^2
    (u = 1.1e-16) of the exact value, so value + 1e-11(||v||^2 + 1) is a certified upper bound on fw_record's
    computed value (measured slack <= 3.5e-9, 0 violations).  Hypotheses whose sample index (t/DT within 1e-9 of
    an integer) or window argmax (top-2 energy tie within 1e-12) is not certifiable fall back to the energy
    bound.  Coarse cells use the 'box' energy bound.  Only nodes whose enclosure reaches the incumbent are
    evaluated with fw_record's call, which in all 360 iterations was exactly the winning node.
  * tcorr (ns, 2) optional station/phase time corrections (used by Component C); with tcorr the certified
    oracle was checked against a dense corrected loop (dense_fw_record_c).

3. IMPLICATIONS FOR SECTION 7.5 / APPENDIX Z
  The statement "the energy of the three-component records in a time window widened by h/Vmin + dtau bounds eta
  over a whole cell" is correct and makes the oracle certifiable, but on this grid it does not make it faster:
  after the first strong event, the Frank-Wolfe maxima are at the noise level, where the energy bound exceeds
  the incumbent almost everywhere.  A practical certified oracle today is "batched evaluation + certified
  floating-point enclosure + exact recheck of the winner" (4.3x faster than fw_record, identical records).
  Pruning that pays in noise-dominated iterations needs subspace-aware cell bounds (the union of the moment-
  tensor subspaces reachable from a cell and the directions of the candidate peak vectors), which remains open.
